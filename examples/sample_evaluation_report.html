Evaluation report: early warning against known disruptions

SupplyLens Early Warning

Of 77 known disruptions that began between 28 Oct 2024 and 30 Jun 2026, the risk index raised an alert for 81% and the static threshold rule for 69%. The index's first alert came a median of 11.5 days after onset, against 13.0 for the static rule; on the 46 disruptions both caught, the index was a median of 1.5 days earlier. False alarms across all entities: 4.0 a month for the index, 2.9 for the static rule.

How alerts are scored

An alert detects a disruption when the same entity is in alert between 14 days before the onset and the end of the disruption. An entity that alerts again within 7 days continues the same alert. An alert that starts outside every such window is a false alarm, unless a port or region the entity depends on is disrupted at the time; those related alerts are left out of precision and of the false-alarm count. Days from onset is the first alert minus the onset; days gained is how much earlier a method was than the static rule on disruptions both caught.

Onset model: will a disruption begin within 14 days?

On 5,787 entity-days of the test period that were not already in a disruption (295 of them within 14 days before one of 22 onsets), the model's ROC AUC is 0.618; using the risk index as the score gives 0.541. Its Brier score is 0.0468 against 0.0485 for always quoting the base rate.

Limits

These results describe one history with a limited number of disruptions, so each rate rests on few events and would differ on another history. Detector settings were chosen on the data scored here. Onsets in a real events table are judgments made afterwards, and the scores depend on them. A rolling baseline treats a change that lasts for months as the new normal and stops alerting on it.
